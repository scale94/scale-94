### The World of False Scarcity

They sell dopamine in micrograms. 60 hertz per minute, a desperate attempt to convert shallow cycles into pulse. Shiny surfaces don't reveal, they conceal; Even your hollow reflection feels expensive. It's a pyramid scheme of pointy pointlessness. Endless upgrades to keep the illusion sharp. Everyone is chasing luxury like it'll solve the ache, but luxury was never about comfort. It was about hierarchy.

At the apex of the triangle of greed, exploitation and narcissism poses a predator. An orange Russian asset.

A predator of pretentiousness risks becoming prey. What does a man of smoke and mirrors desire? Seraphine wondered. A thick stick of dried leaves – rolled into an illusion of status.

He was scrolling his favorite social media app, when his orange head suddenly turned red. His old political rival was showing off his latest acquisition: live on stream.

"Thanks for tuning in to witness this very special moment. I feel honored to share it with you. After years of struggles the stars finally aligned. Everybody told me the only Dragomel cigar still left in existence was in the hands of a very private collector, with no interest in selling."

He tossed his phone against the wall like it owed him gratification. This wasn't about tobacco. It was about totalitarianism.

His splinter-fractured ego reflected in the sections of the display which didn't crack. He dialed the number of his dirty-laundry man.

"Smith has it!"
"Has what?"
"The Dragomel!" His assistant had no idea what he was talking about, but admitting it would result in even more toddler rage, so he played along.
"You better get it for me, if you don't want to be fired!"

The threat landed somewhere between kindergarten and cartel. The line went dead, right after the phone hit the wall a second time.

Vance Keller had a bunch of offshore contacts and no moral spine. Not ideal traits for stealing a roll of luxurious leaves from the hands of someone like Smith. He had to act quickly before Orange got his entitled hands on a new phone. 12 to 36 hours, depending on how long it took another poor assistant to find a new iPhone dipped in gold and diamonds.

Keller messaged six cartel members, two arms dealers and one ex-Bolivian diplomat. Nobody feared the Dragomel. But Smith had the kind of reputation that made war criminals wait politely in hotel lobbies.

Even the ones who moved uranium and organs through diplomatic channels suddenly developed principles. So it became another day of questionable lobby activity: sniffing overpriced cocaine in a 5-star bathroom. Pretending to be a diplomat and trying to convince hardened criminals, mediocre pay was worth ending up dead in a ditch.

"Hit me up, when you need more coke. My shit fucks you up way better, than tobacco ever could."

The last questionable figure left Keller with dubious wisdom and no solution. The only thing about to get dissolved was Keller's nasal cavity.

"Your nose is bleeding," she handed him a piece of toilet paper.
"What are you doing in the men's bathroom?"
"Could ask you the same question, but your trunk already told that story."
"If you can't help me with.." Keller bit his tongue, he almost slipped.
"The Dragomel you want me to fuck off?"
"Who told you about that?" the cocaine weighed heavier than his caution.
"Your nose isn't the only thing leaking."

Keller swallowed a mix of pride and blood.
"You…" his voice cracked like a rock of crack combusting into paranoia. Seraphine was already dissolving into smoke.

Keller's mood plummeted to the basement floor, until he flipped the toilet paper. Below the crusty blood cells, something to type into his trusty cell phone.

He jabbed the number with a trembling thumb, crimson smudge smearing across the glass.

*Ring. Ring.*

"You are calling outside of our business hours. If you are addicted to cocaine and need more please dial one. If your ego has suffered recent structural damage, press two to find out who is mocking you."

He hung up in anger. He stared at his phone like it owed him a fix and a fixer.
