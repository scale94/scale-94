## Description

Post-Brexit data protection reform weakening UK GDPR protections to enable algorithmic management of workers, reducing automated decision-making safeguards and removing 'balancing tests' for data processing while maintaining EU adequacy status through 2025.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application to all data subjects [+1], administrative penalties for non-compliance [+0.5], enables permanent algorithmic decision databases [+1], explicit chilling effect on worker organizing through weakened ADM protections [+1], mission creep to AI training and 'recognized legitimate interests' [+1], removed judicial oversight for many data processing decisions [+0.5], enables digital exclusion through automated decision-making [+0.5]. Total: 4.5/7 → 4/5

## Implementation Notes

Royal Assent granted 19 June 2025; provisions phasing in through secondary legislation over 12 months. Replaces abandoned DPDI Bill with reduced but still significant weakening of data rights. Key change: Automated decision-making protections now limited to 'special category data' (race, health, etc.) only, allowing AI management tools to fire gig workers or schedule shifts without human review if based on 'performance data.' Creates 'recognized legitimate interests' bypassing balancing tests for data processing. EU adequacy status extended to December 2025 pending review of these changes. Labour government claims 'pro-innovation' approach but removes Article 22 GDPR protections that prevented solely automated decisions with legal/significant effects. Union concerns (TUC) about algorithmic management in warehouses, ride-share, and platform work largely ignored.
