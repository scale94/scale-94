## Description

Belgium implements data retention using CJEU 'targeted retention' exception where the 'specific, crime-affected area' has the same borders as Belgian state territory - effectively general retention.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application (entire territory as 'targeted area') [+1], no judicial oversight for retention [+1], irreversible data collection (metadata databases) [+1], mission creep using CJEU 'targeted retention' exception [+1], digital exclusion not applicable [0]. Total: 4/5

## Implementation Notes

EXPLOITING CJEU LOOPHOLE FOR MASS SURVEILLANCE. Legal basis: Article 15(1) ePrivacy Directive allows targeted retention in 'specific, particularly crime-prone areas.' Belgian implementation: The 'specific area' = entire Belgian territory. This follows CJEU La Quadrature du Net II (2024) ruling that allowed targeted retention without national security threat. CJEU also permitted universal IP address retention for serious crime. Belgium's approach: Creative interpretation of 'targeted' to mean 'national.' Similar to France's rotating short-term retention claiming continuous national security threat. Concerns: (1) Circumvents CJEU Digital Rights Ireland ruling against general retention; (2) No transparency about what data retained, for how long; (3) Law enforcement access procedures unclear; (4) Creates surveillance infrastructure for social control. Opposition: EDRi and digital rights groups criticizing 'creative' interpretations of CJEU exceptions. Verfassungsblog analysis documents exploitation of loopholes. Workers' concern: Mass surveillance of communications metadata enables mapping of worker networks, union organizing, protest planning. Location data particularly sensitive for tracking worker movements.
