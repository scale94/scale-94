## Description

Federal legislation allowing FTC to sue platforms failing to 'prevent harms' to minors, creating incentives for invasive age verification and content filtering that disproportionately targets LGBTQ+ youth and young worker organizers while undermining Section 230 protections.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application to minors but affects all users through platform over-compliance [+1], no direct criminal penalties for users but FTC enforcement creates liability [+0.5], irreversible age verification data collection incentivized [+1], severe chilling effect on youth organizing and LGBTQ+ worker communities [+1], mission creep from 'harms' to broad content filtering evident [+1], FTC discretion without judicial oversight [+0.5], digital exclusion of youth from organizing spaces [+1]. Total: 5/7 → 4/5

## Implementation Notes

Passed Senate July 2024 (91-3) but stalled in House; reintroduced 2025 as S. 1748 and incorporated into broader KIDS Act package. March 2026 House Energy & Commerce Committee hearings show continued bipartisan support but Democratic opposition over lack of 'duty of care' and state preemption. While 2025 version removed explicit 'duty of care' language, creates liability for 'serious emotional disturbance' and 'anxiety' that platforms will interpret through over-filtering. Heritage Foundation and Project 2025 allies support bill; incoming FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson (Trump appointee) has vowed to 'fight back against the trans agenda,' creating acute risk of weaponization against LGBTQ+ worker organizing content. 90+ civil liberties organizations oppose.
