# Camera Surveillance Act Amendments

## Lag (2018:1200) om kamerabevakning (amended 2025)

## Description

Amendments effective April 1, 2025 remove permit requirement from Privacy Protection Authority (IMY) for camera surveillance. Organizations now only need to conduct and document self-assessed 'balancing of interests.' Expands law enforcement surveillance powers including facial recognition for identifying 'gang members' and automatic number plate recognition.

## Severity Rationale

> Broad application to public spaces and workplaces [+0.5], no criminal penalties for subjects [+0], biometric facial recognition capabilities expanded [+0.5], chilling effect on public anonymity and protest [+1], mission creep to law enforcement and private sector [+0.5], self-assessment replaces permit requirement [+0.5], digital exclusion through pervasive surveillance [+0]. Total: 3/5 (Medium)

## Implementation Notes

Part of government 'video surveillance offensive' against gang violence. Police camera target increased from 1,600 to 2,500 cameras (fivefold increase from 2022). Self-assessment replaces independent oversight - organizations must document 'legitimate interest assessment' but no external review required before deployment. Facial recognition explicitly expanded for police to identify gang members. Drone surveillance powers increased. Exemptions from notice signs for unmarked police cars. Part of Tidö Agreement between Sweden Democrats, Moderates, Christian Democrats, and Liberals.
