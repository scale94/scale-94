## Description

Belgium's dominant digital identity system used by 90% of adults for banking, government services, and private sector authentication. eIDAS High certification level. Originally private sector (banks/telcos), now partially state-owned (FPIM since 2021), eIDAS notified 2024.

## Severity Rationale

> Near-universal application (7M+ users, 90% of Belgian adults) [+1], biometric collection for high-assurance [+0.5], mission creep to cross-border EU use [+0.5], digital exclusion (requires smartphone/bank account) [+1], no criminal penalties [0], judicial oversight not applicable [0]. Total: 3/5

## Implementation Notes

DE FACTO MANDATORY DIGITAL ID. While technically voluntary, itsme is: (1) Used by 90% of Belgian adult residents; (2) Required for most online banking; (3) Used for government services via MyGov.be; (4) Expanded to 30 European countries for cross-border authentication; (5) Now accepted by INPI France for company formalities. Corporate structure: Founded by Belgian banks (KBC, ING, Belfius, BNP Paribas Fortis) and telecoms (Telenet, Proximus, Orange). FPIM (Belgian government investment fund) acquired stake 2021 - partial nationalization. Acquired Dutch iDIN in 2025 - expansion to Netherlands. Concerns: (1) Private sector control of essential identity infrastructure; (2) Biometric verification (fingerprint/face) for high-assurance transactions; (3) Data stored in Europe but corporate profit motive; (4) Network effects make it effectively mandatory; (5) Expansion to employment/HR verification. Opposition: Limited civil society opposition due to perceived convenience. Privacy by design claims but centralization concerns. Workers' concern: Required for accessing employment platforms, banking, social services - undocumented workers face exclusion. Union perspective: Convenient but dependency on private infrastructure problematic.
