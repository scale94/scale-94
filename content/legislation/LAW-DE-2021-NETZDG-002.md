# NetzDG BKA Reporting Amendment (ZMI)

## Description

Amendment to Network Enforcement Act requiring social media platforms to proactively report 'manifestly criminal content' to Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) Central Reporting Office (ZMI), creating direct pipeline from platform moderation to federal criminal investigations.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application to social media users [+1], platforms face €50M fines driving over-compliance [+0.5], irreversible police database entries for reported content [+1], severe chilling effect on political organizing and union activity [+1], mission creep from hate speech to 'economic sabotage' and protest organizing [+1], no judicial oversight before BKA referral [+1], digital exclusion through platform fear [+0.5]. Total: 5/7 → 4/5

## Implementation Notes

Active since February 2022. ZMI (Zentralstelle zur Aufnahme von Strafinhalten im Internet) processes ~53,800 notices (2021-2025), projecting 250,000 annual submissions and 150,000 criminal proceedings. Platforms must report within specific timeframes, creating 'overblocking' to avoid €50M fines. Content includes not just hate speech but 'incitement,' 'public disorder,' and 'economic sabotage' - categories applicable to union organizing and protest coordination. BKA performs 'initial legal check' without judicial oversight before referring to state prosecutors. Partner portals (HessenGegenHetze, REspect!, state media authorities) funnel complaints to federal police, creating state-funded informant ecosystem. Worker organizing communications on Facebook, Instagram, X subject to automated scanning and police reporting. Historical context: East German Stasi legacy makes German left particularly sensitive to state surveillance of speech. NetzDG amended 2024 to accommodate EU DSA but retained BKA reporting. Civil liberties groups (GFF, CCC) warn of 'speech policing within criminal justice machinery' and chilling effect on political discourse.
