# Data Storage and Access to Electronic Information Proposal

## Ju2024/02286 Datalagring och åtkomst till elektronisk information (Proposal)

## Description

Proposed law would force companies to store and provide law enforcement access to users' communications, including end-to-end encrypted messages. Creates encryption backdoor requirement - criticized by 230+ organizations as creating vulnerabilities criminals can exploit. Swedish Armed Forces warned it 'cannot be fulfilled without introducing vulnerabilities and backdoors.'

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application to all encrypted communications users [+1], criminal penalties for non-compliance by providers [+1], forces creation of encryption backdoors [+1], severe chilling effect on all private communications [+1], mission creep from serious crime to all investigations [+0.5], warrantless access to encrypted communications [+1], digital exclusion as encrypted services may leave market [+0.5]. Total: 5/5 (Extreme)

## Implementation Notes

Open letter signed by 230+ organizations and individuals from 50+ countries including Statewatch, Global Encryption Coalition, and cybersecurity experts. Swedish Armed Forces publicly opposed: 'cannot be fulfilled without introducing vulnerabilities and backdoors that third parties can exploit.' Would force platforms to choose between undermining security or leaving Swedish market. Over 40% of Swedish internet users rely on end-to-end encrypted messaging. Part of coordinated EU push - similar proposals in France (removed from final law) and UK (Apple appeal). G7 states declared intention to grant police access to encrypted communications despite expert consensus it's impossible without undermining security for all.
