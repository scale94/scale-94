# iDIN Acquisition by itsme (Dutch Digital Identity Transition)

## Description

Dutch bank-based digital identity system iDIN (operational since 2014) being acquired by Belgian itsme. Transition begins spring 2026, full integration by 2028. 300 connected websites, 15M identification actions in 2024.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application for Dutch bank customers (15M identification actions 2024) [+1], biometric collection via itsme platform [+0.5], mission creep from banking to cross-border services [+0.5], digital exclusion (bank account required) [+1], no criminal penalties [0]. Total: 3/5

## Implementation Notes

PRIVATIZATION AND CONSOLIDATION OF DIGITAL ID. Background: (1) iDIN created 2014 by Dutch banks (ING, Rabobank, ABN Amro, ASN Bank) via Currence; (2) 300 websites connected; (3) 15M identification actions 2024. Acquisition: (1) itsme (Belgian) acquiring iDIN 2025; (2) Transition spring 2026 - June 2026 dual operation; (3) Full integration 2028. Implications: (1) Consolidation of European digital identity under single corporate entity; (2) itsme now controls both Belgian and Dutch bank-based identity; (3) Cross-border expansion - itsme already in 30 European countries; (4) Aligns with eIDAS 2.0 requirements. Concerns: (1) Private sector monopoly over essential identity infrastructure; (2) Foreign (Belgian) control of Dutch critical infrastructure; (3) Profit motive conflicts with public interest; (4) Biometric data centralization; (5) Creates single point of failure/surveillance. Opposition: Limited public debate. Banking sector framing as 'future-proof solution.' No civil society opposition documented. Workers' concern: Bank-based identity creates barriers for unbanked workers, migrants, gig economy workers without traditional employment. itsme expansion enables cross-border worker surveillance by platforms.
