# Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act (Bill C-8)

## An Act respecting cyber security, amending the Telecommunications Act (Bill C-8)

## Description

Reintroduced cybersecurity legislation granting Communications Security Establishment (CSE) and ministers warrantless powers to compel telecom data, access critical infrastructure systems, and issue secret 'cyber security directions' with potential for encryption backdoors and Five Eyes intelligence sharing.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application to telecom users and critical infrastructure [+1], criminal and administrative penalties ($15M/day for corporations) [+1], irreversible CSE data retention and Five Eyes sharing [+1], explicit targeting of infrastructure workers and potential chilling effect on strikes [+1], mission creep from cyber security to intelligence collection explicit [+1], warrantless Minister powers remove judicial oversight [+1], creates digital exclusion through surveillance infrastructure [+0.5]. Total: 5.5/7 → 5/5

## Implementation Notes

Reintroduced June 18, 2025 as Bill C-8 after predecessor Bill C-26 died on order paper due to 2025 prorogation. Near-identical to C-26 but removes Canada Evidence Act amendments and adds limited appeal mechanisms. Part I (Telecommunications Act): Minister of Industry can compel 'any information' from telecoms without warrant and share with CSE/CSIS. Citizen Lab warns sections 15.2(2)(c), (l), (m) could force telecoms to 'install backdoors inside Canada's networks by weakening encryption.' Part II (CCSPA): Designated operators (energy, transport, finance, telecom) must report incidents within 72 hours to CSE and comply with secret 'cyber security directions' that cannot be disclosed publicly. CSE can repurpose cyber security data for foreign intelligence collection. Information shared with Five Eyes partners (US, UK, AU, NZ) without additional safeguards. Privacy Commissioner warned of 'warrantless search powers' and 'far-reaching information-sharing without individuals' awareness.' Intelligence Commissioner Simon Noël testified that powers bypass CSE Act oversight mechanisms. Infrastructure unions (energy, telecom, transport) subject to surveillance during job actions; no exemption for labor disputes. EU data adequacy status at risk due to surveillance provisions.
