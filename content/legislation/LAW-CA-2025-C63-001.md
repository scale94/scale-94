# Online Harms Act (Bill C-63)

## Description

Proposed legislation establishing Digital Safety Commission with powers to mandate age verification and platform scanning for child protection, reintroduced after dying on order paper with 2024 prorogation.

## Severity Rationale

> Universal application to online platforms [+1], no criminal penalties for users but platform liability [+0.5], age verification data collection potentially permanent [+0.5], chilling effect on speech through 'duty to protect children' [+0.5], mission creep to 'harmful content' expansion likely [+0.5], Digital Safety Commission oversight (not judicial) [+0.5], digital exclusion through age verification barriers [+0.5]. Total: 4/7 → 3/5

## Implementation Notes

Died on order paper 2024; reintroduced in modified form 2025 as Bill C-216 (private member's bill) and government signaling 'fresh look.' Controversial provisions include 'digital safety duty' requiring platforms to restrict content harmful to children, which may mandate age verification. Canadian Centre for Child Protection advocating for explicit age assurance requirements. Unlike UK Online Safety Act, C-63 did not explicitly mandate encryption breaking, but left regulatory authority to require 'design features' that could include client-side scanning.
