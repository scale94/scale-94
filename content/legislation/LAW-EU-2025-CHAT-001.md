# Chat Control / Child Sexual Abuse Material Regulation (CSAR)

## Proposal for a Regulation to Prevent and Combat Child Sexual Abuse (2022/0155(COD))

## Description

Would mandate client-side scanning of all private messages, emails, and cloud content before encryption, effectively breaking end-to-end encryption. Forces platforms to scan for CSAM using AI and hash matching, with mandatory age verification eliminating anonymous communication. Creates infrastructure for mass surveillance disguised as child protection. — German political context: German government split on supporting EU-wide 'Chat Control' regulation mandating client-side scanning of encrypted messages for CSAM detection, with current draft including mandatory age verification and voluntary but incentivized mass scanning.

## Implementation Notes

TRILOGUE NEGOTIATIONS ONGOING as of March 2025. Council position (Nov 2025) removed mandatory detection orders but keeps 'voluntary' scanning and risk mitigation framework. Parliament position protects encryption. Germany, Netherlands, Austria, Poland opposed mandatory scanning. 230+ civil society organizations signed open letter opposing. European Parliament study found 80% false positive rates in some jurisdictions. Timeline: Final vote expected mid-2026. If passed, takes immediate binding effect across all 27 member states without national implementing legislation.

[German context] Justice Minister Hubig opposes mass scanning but Interior Ministry leads negotiations and may support watered-down version. November 2025 Council position removed mandatory scanning but retained voluntary scanning incentives and mandatory age verification. Historical parallel to East German Stasi surveillance creates unique political sensitivity. If passed, would require scanning of WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage. German Constitutional Court previously struck down data retention; similar challenge likely. Worker communications currently protected by strong labor privacy laws would be exposed to employer-accessible platform surveillance.

[Status update - March 2026] Danish presidency backed down from mandatory scanning in October 2025 after sustained public pressure. European Parliament LIBE Committee voted to exclude end-to-end encryption from scanning requirements. However, 'backdoor attempt' continues via 'upload moderation' wording being pushed. Interim 'Chat Control 1.0' (voluntary scanning) extended until April 2026. 80%+ of public consultation respondents opposed the regulation. Corporate beneficiaries: Thorn, Microsoft PhotoDNA providers, AI scanning vendors. Workers' concern: Proposal destroys secure worker communication channels, enabling employer surveillance of union organizing via platform-level access.
